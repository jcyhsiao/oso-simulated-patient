Treatment

Goals: to shorten the clinical course and provide pain relief, and prevent complications of joint

Patient stated, “tried Tylenol and it was not effective”. (should discontinue)

She is experiencing acute mild/moderate pain / Gout attack.

Start Meds within 24 hours is best.

Pain Management: may prescribe NSAID, Steroids or Colchicine. Does not need combination therapy. This is a first-time attack of mild to moderate Gout (only needs monotherapy. 

Best Treatment in this case would be NSAID’s, (ibuprofen 800 mg three to four times daily or indomethacin 25 to 50 mg four times daily. 

 OR: Prednisone 0.5 mg/kg/day PO x 5-10 days (30mg per day, if given for 5 to 7 days will not need to taper) give with food.

OR: Colchicine 1.2 milligrams (mg) at the first sign of a gout attack, followed by 0.6 mg after 1 hour. On subsequent days, Colchicine 0.6 mg twice a day for an additional 48 hours following relief of the flare up. 

Topical Cream: Can prescribe in addition to above but no deduction if doesn’t. Topical analgesics can be applied to help with pain but not necessarily inflammation in addition to the above (Voltaren cream)

Should not order ASA  

    

If analgesic needed: could use Voltaren topically – does not help with inflammation or joint complications but can help alleviate symptomatic pain. 

Start Lisinopril 10mg (ACE). 

Stop HCTZ – contributory to Gout attack
