# Interprofessional Simulated Patient Scenario

## Patient Introduction
- **Patient Name:** Linda Johnson
- **Age:** 67 years old
- **Living Situation:** Widowed, lives alone in a second-floor apartment without an elevator
- **Occupation:** Retired elementary school teacher
- **Insurance:** Medicare with limited supplemental coverage

### Chief Concern (Patient Statement)
"I've been having trouble getting around since I fell a few months ago. I'm tired all the time, my mouth hurts when I eat, and I don't feel like myself anymore."

## Medical History
- Type 2 Diabetes (poorly controlled)
- Hypertension
- Osteoarthritis of knees and hands
- Mild depression following spouse's death 18 months ago
- Obesity (BMI 33)
- History of one fall 3 months ago
- Diabetic peripheral neuropathy
- Reports occasional dizziness when standing

## Current Medications
- Metformin
- Lisinopril
- Atorvastatin
- Gabapentin
- Sertraline

## Social History
- Lives alone
- Daughter lives two hours away
- Transportation difficulties
- Limited income
- Occasionally skips medications due to cost
- Eats mostly soft processed foods because chewing is uncomfortable
- Has not visited a dentist in 5 years
- Reports increasing social isolation

## Presenting Symptoms
The patient reports:
- Difficulty chewing due to painful gums and loose teeth
- Dry mouth
- Poor appetite
- Recent 10-pound unintended weight loss
- Difficulty climbing stairs
- Fear of falling again
- Fatigue
- Difficulty managing diabetes
- Feeling lonely and overwhelmed

## Simulated Patient Interview Information
The patient should reveal information gradually rather than all at once.

### If asked about oral health
- "I brush once a day. My gums bleed when I brush."
- "Some of my teeth feel loose."
- "My mouth is always dry."
- "It hurts to chew meats and vegetables."

### If asked about mobility
- "I'm afraid I'm going to fall again."
- "My knees hurt all the time."
- "I hold onto the furniture when I walk around my apartment."

### If asked about medications
- "Sometimes I skip medications because they're expensive."
- "I get confused keeping track of everything."

### If asked about mental health
- "I don't get out much anymore."
- "Since my husband died I've been pretty down."
- "Some days I just don't feel motivated."

### If asked about nutrition
- "I mostly eat soup, mashed potatoes, and soft foods."
- "Fresh foods are hard to chew and expensive."

## Findings Available to Learners

### Vital Signs
- **BP:** 152/92
- **Pulse:** 88
- **Blood glucose (recent):** Elevated
- **HbA1c:** 9.2%

### Oral Findings
- Generalized moderate-to-severe periodontal disease
- Heavy plaque accumulation
- Gingival inflammation with bleeding
- Multiple carious lesions
- Xerostomia
- Ill-fitting partial denture

## Profession-Specific Roles

### Dental Hygiene
**Should identify:**
- Periodontal disease
- Oral hygiene deficits
- Xerostomia concerns
- Impact of oral health on nutrition and diabetes
- Preventive education needs

**Potential recommendations:**
- Periodontal therapy
- Oral hygiene instruction
- Dry-mouth management strategies
- Caries prevention plan
- Interprofessional communication regarding diabetes control

### Dentistry
**Should identify:**
- Restorative treatment needs
- Cararies management
- Loose teeth and prognosis
- Denture evaluation
- Impact of oral disease on overall health

**Potential recommendations:**
- Comprehensive dental examination
- Restorative care
- Extraction evaluation if indicated
- Prosthetic consultation

### Medicine
**Should identify:**
- Poor diabetic control
- Fall risk
- Polypharmacy concerns
- Hypertension management
- Depression screening needs

**Potential recommendations:**
- Medication review
- Diabetes management adjustment
- Further diagnostic testing
- Referrals to rehabilitation services

### Nursing
**Should identify:**
- Medication adherence barriers
- Self-management challenges
- Patient education needs
- Chronic disease monitoring

**Potential recommendations:**
- Diabetes education
- Medication management strategies
- Home safety education
- Health coaching

### Occupational Therapy (OT)
**Should identify:**
- Challenges with activities of daily living
- Difficulty organizing medications
- Home safety concerns
- Energy conservation needs

**Potential recommendations:**
- Home modification assessment
- Adaptive equipment
- Medication management systems
- Functional independence strategies

### Physical Therapy (PT)
**Should identify:**
- Impaired balance
- Fear of falling
- Lower-extremity weakness
- Reduced mobility

**Potential recommendations:**
- Strengthening program
- Balance training
- Gait assessment
- Fall-prevention interventions

### Social Work
**Should identify:**
- Social isolation
- Financial barriers
- Transportation limitations
- Mental health concerns

**Potential recommendations:**
- Community resources
- Transportation assistance
- Mental health support
- Medication assistance programs
- Senior services referral

## Team Challenge
The interprofessional team must answer:
1. What are the patient's top three immediate concerns?
2. How do oral health, diabetes, nutrition, mobility, and mental health interact in this case?
3. What profession-specific interventions are needed?
4. What referrals should be prioritized?
5. What patient-centered goals can the team establish for the next 3 months?

## Example Patient-Centered Goals
- Reduce risk of falls and improve confidence with mobility.
- Improve oral health and eliminate pain when eating.
- Improve diabetes control (reduce HbA1c).
- Increase social engagement and community support.
- Improve medication adherence.
- Improve nutritional intake.

This case works well for a 30–45 minute small-group IPE event because every profession has a clearly defined role while also seeing how oral health, chronic disease management, mobility, mental health, and social determinants of health are interconnected.
