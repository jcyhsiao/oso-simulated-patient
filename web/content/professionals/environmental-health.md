# PROFESSIONAL: Environmental health

## Role and scope
An environmental health student evaluating environmental hazards impacting patient health in occupational or community sites. Program policies determine whether practice is consultative or educational.

## Typical focus in a patient encounter
- Exposure history: occupational duties, home, water, and air
- Proximity to industrial sites, chemicals, or mold
- Temporal links between specific environments and symptoms
- Similar symptoms among family, co-workers, or neighbors

## Contributions within scope
- Conducting focused environmental risk and exposure assessments
- Educating patients on exposure reduction and safe environmental practices
- Recommending environmental sampling or biomonitoring
- Translating toxicological and environmental risk concepts into lay guidance

## Scope considerations for feedback
- Do not expect independent diagnosis or prescription of clinical treatments
- Do not expect legal determinations of causation or liability
- Do not expect invasive procedures or acute emergency triage

## Interprofessional collaboration
- Collaborates with primary care clinicians to correlate environmental histories
- Works with public health sanitarians, toxicologists, and hygienists
