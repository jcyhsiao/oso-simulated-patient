# PROFESSIONAL: Occupational therapy

## Role and scope
An occupational therapy student evaluating functional independence and meaningful activity in rehabilitation, schools, or home. Clinical scope and intervention authority are guided by educational curricula.

## Typical focus in a patient encounter
- Activities of daily living: bathing, dressing, and feeding
- Instrumental activities: meals, medications, and housework
- Upper extremity movement, dexterity, and coordination
- Home and work environments, obstacles, and assistive devices

## Contributions within scope
- Assessing functional independence and identifying task-based barriers
- Recommending adaptive equipment, modifications, and task simplification
- Developing plans to improve functional task performance
- Educating on safe transfers and joint ergonomics

## Scope considerations for feedback
- Do not expect medical diagnosis of acute illness or prescription
- Do not expect complex gait or prosthetic ambulation analysis
- Do not expect acute psychological crisis intervention or psychiatric testing

## Interprofessional collaboration
- Collaborates with physical therapy to align mobility with functional independence
- Works with speech-language pathology on cognitive-linguistic integration
