# Level 3 OSCE activity

Educational reference: Zayyan M. Objective Structured Clinical Examination: The Assessment of Choice. Oman Medical Journal. 2011;26:219–222. [Article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3191703/). This historical overview describes conventional OSCE design; it does not validate AI assessment.

Use [evidence-and-review.md](evidence-and-review.md) for AMEE quality-assurance guidance and AI-specific limitations. Blueprint objectives to station tasks and criteria; review prebriefing, post-assessment learning, examiner consistency, accessibility and technical-failure procedures. Review random behavior for comparability. These are authoring review decisions, not capabilities activated by selecting this template.

Source-informed foundation: OSCEs use stations with specified tasks, allocated time and predetermined assessment criteria. Design aligns station skills with learner level and assessment aims. Candidate instructions, consistent patient portrayal, examiner preparation and trial runs support delivery. A single patient encounter can represent one station rather than an entire OSCE.

Project authoring decisions: extract the actual station task, timing, allowed resources, rubric/version, completion conditions, assistance rules and feedback timing from faculty directions. Leave missing values unresolved. Do not impose a universal station duration, station count, passing score, checklist or rescue rule.

Keep the patient unaware of concealed criteria. Preserve authorized finding delivery. Do not assume real-time coaching is permitted. Flag prompt-level random variation for explicit review of comparability. State which skills the chosen medium cannot demonstrate.

Produce a draft station specification and unresolved decisions. Automated scoring, timing enforcement and examination validity require separate implementation/evidence; selecting this template establishes none of them.
