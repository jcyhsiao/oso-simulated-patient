# Evidence and design review

Reviewed October 22, 2026. This is evidence-informed guidance, not validation or endorsement of this pipeline. The four-level architecture and source-folder arrangement are project choices; literature supports educational processes rather than this exact file structure.

## Simulation education

[INACSL Healthcare Simulation Standards of Best Practice](https://www.inacsl.org/healthcare-simulation-standards-of-best-practice) identify measurable objectives, purposeful design, preparation/briefing, facilitation and a planned debriefing process. That process may involve feedback, guided reflection or debriefing; cornerstone standards were revised in 2025. The official overview was reviewed, not every full standard.

Project application: align the task with objectives and observable evidence. Plan orientation and post-encounter learning before educational deployment. Explain the medium's limitations, ground rules, finding access and assistance expectations. For assessment, specify feedback timing instead of assuming immediate answer disclosure. Missing plans remain explicit authoring gaps; the builder does not currently enforce them.

## Standardized patients

[Lewis et al., ASPE Standards of Best Practice, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41077-017-0043-4) address human role players, case development, consistent portrayal and preparation for feedback/assessment. Portrayal, feedback and instrument completion are distinct skills; standardization depends on context.

Project adaptation: review each role independently, test unexpected questions and compare repeated portrayals. Our separation of patient dialogue from coaching is an implementation choice, not an ASPE prohibition against trained SP feedback. Human-SP standards do not validate AI role performance. Review representation and accessibility with intended users.

## OSCE assessment

[Khan et al., AMEE Guide 81 Part II, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23968324/) emphasizes quality assurance, examiner/SP preparation, suitable rubrics, realistic tasks and alignment with programme outcomes. Its abstract was reviewed. [Zayyan, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3191703/) remains introductory background rather than the sole design authority.

Project application: map objectives to tasks and criteria; review assistance, technical-failure procedures, accommodations and comparability. A single encounter does not demonstrate a complete dependable examination or valid pass/fail decisions. Prompts do not provide reliable timing enforcement. Review randomness, including Shannon's recall policy, before assessment use; retain the existing practice behavior until an explicit adaptation is approved.

## Feedback and practice

[Cook et al., 2013 systematic review/meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22938677/) found benefits for several simulation design features, including feedback and distributed practice, with substantial heterogeneity. It excluded studies solely involving computer-based virtual patients or human actors; application here is indirect. The repetitive-practice estimate alone was not statistically conclusive.

Project application: support targeted practice and evidence-grounded feedback when authorized. Evaluate performance rather than satisfaction alone. Do not claim mastery learning without a defensible proficiency standard and remediation process.

## AI-specific evidence

[Gao et al., 2026](https://mededu.jmir.org/2026/1/e95578/) reported moderate overall AI–faculty scoring agreement, weaker for communication, and did not support unsupervised high-stakes scoring. These single-site findings do not establish universal performance.

Project application: separately evaluate factual fidelity, portrayal, disclosure, feedback and scoring. Current automated scoring remains disabled. Record model/host/version, inspect live pilot transcripts, and review accessibility and fairness. Code tests cannot establish these outcomes.

## Review record

For each intended use, record objective/task alignment, prebrief plan, finding/release map, portrayal review, assistance rules, post-encounter plan, technical/accessibility limits, unresolved source conflicts, faculty review and live-pilot findings. Distinguish proposed, reviewed, locally checked and live-tested items. Development gaps must remain visible when judging readiness. These review criteria are not all enforced by the current build.
